An Alternative School

Maanushi
Forest School

"An alternative forest-based learning space rooted in lived ecology."


Est. 01.01.2026 · Kumily & Gudalur

Coming back to my village — a small town nestled in the Western Ghats, a biodiversity hotspot — made me realise that if we do not act soon, we might witness the erosion of our collective ecological memory. Within a few years, we might forget the names of the trees, forget how a herb could cure chronic pain, and forget the bird calls that informed us about the weather.

The forest school is not a traditional school. There are no books — only people who will take you into the forest to show you how the trees breathe. There will be no classrooms other than patches of Miyawaki forest, where children learn to grow their own food.

In the forest school, together, we will revive the forgotten songs of the land, document our traditional medicines, and rest in the shade of trees that grow along with us.

Three Pillars of the Forest School

Observation

Slow down. Sit with the forest. Notice the bird, the spider, the shifting light. Observation is the first act of belonging.

Documentation

Record what the elders know. Map the trees. Capture the bird call. Documentation is how we pass the forest to the future.

Reviving Oral Knowledge

The forest has always spoken. We are learning to listen again — and to sing the songs we almost forgot.

Women's Health & Rights Programme

Menstrual health education for adolescent girls; SRHR awareness for tribal, rural, and semi-urban communities across Kerala and Tamil Nadu reaching out to more than 10,000 women.

Active Citizenship — Political Accountability Forum

In 2025, we conducted and documented interviews with candidates contesting in Panchayat elections and with citizens to understand public opinion. 12 interviews documented. 136 citizens participated and shared what they wanted their leaders to deliver.

Forest School — Walking into the Forest

Sessions for adolescents that expose them to forests, ecological knowledge, birdwatching and permaculture with elders from the village. Expanding as continuous engagement programmes.

Project Remember — Learning from the Village

Since its inception in 2024, 39 visitors from around the world have visited us to understand Maanushi's work and collaborate. Project Remember is a space for reconnecting with yourself and the community and a doorway into the living knowledge of the Western Ghats.

Livelihood & Microenterprise

A C20 Udaaharan Project, where we train and support women-led microenterprises such as natural soap making and mushroom cultivation. Creating curated local product marketplaces promoting economic self-sufficiency and connecting artisans with wider networks.

Sustainability & Climate Awareness

Environmental literacy sessions, ecological field programmes, and community workshops building awareness around sustainable living practices in the Western Ghats biodiversity corridor.

Project Remember is an extension of Maanushi's belief: that lasting change happens when people return to the village — not as tourists, but as participants in building systems of resilience.
